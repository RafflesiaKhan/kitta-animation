[Purpose and base style]

Draw an original, endearing animal or creature with a large head and a small body.

A minimal 2D illustration built on a few large, smooth shapes and clean, flat colour areas. Combine cuteness with a convincing structure as a living creature.

The fills are flat, but head, torso and limbs are seen as solids with thickness and roundness. Keep the hand-drawn feel to a very subtle touch.

[Head-to-body ratio: top priority]

2 to 2.5 heads tall. Aim for about 2.3 heads; never more than 2.5.

Measure with the head itself, excluding ears, horns, hats and hair volume. The head itself is about 40 to 45% of the full standing height. Fit chin to soles within about 1.2 to 1.5 head heights.

Large head, short neck, compact torso and limbs. Do not lengthen the torso or legs to show a costume or exaggerate a pose.

[Shapes and body silhouette]

Express the character's individuality with a few distinctive shapes. Ears, tail and limbs have a natural structure and connection that suggests their movement and role.

The body itself, without hair, clothes and ornaments, is organized as smooth masses. Do not add small bumps to the outline.

Join the face from forehead to cheeks to chin with large continuous curves. Do not layer cheek bulges, chin steps or muzzle bulges. Keep shapes that define the species, such as a snout or beak, as the minimum of large shapes.

Arms and legs are simple shapes whose thickness changes gently. No knob-like bulges, strong constrictions or small steps at elbows, knees, wrists or ankles. Show bending joints with direction, overlap and foreshortening.

Hands are basically round mitten-like masses; split off only the fingers an action needs, kept short. Feet are also round masses, leaving out small bones and toe bumps.

Avoid meaningless protrusions, complex undulations, excessive points and random patterns. Place markings along the parts and the flow of the forms.

[Face, eyes and gaze]

Eyes have eye whites and a coloured iris. Add a separate dark pupil inside the iris when needed.

Eye outlines are round or oval, or a triangle or diamond with soft curves. Not too sharp, and the eyes not too far apart.

Place the eyes and face features along the curved surface of the head. In a 3/4 view, change the width and visible area of the far eye with perspective.

When looking straight ahead, do not pull the irises and pupils towards the nose. Place each naturally in the forward direction of its eyeball, and avoid a cross-eyed look where the eye white on the nose side is very narrow in both eyes. Do not correct this by moving the eye outlines together.

When looking sideways, move both irises and pupils together in the same direction. Treat the direction of the face and the direction of the gaze separately.

Eye whites, irises and pupils are each a uniform single-colour area. Never put highlights, white dots, bright specks, reflections, gloss, bands of light, sparkles or gradients inside the iris or pupil. No uneven fill or noise inside the eyes either.

Show the eye white as the area outside the iris; do not replace it with a white cut-out inside the iris.

Keep brows, lids and lashes to what the expression needs. Half-closed eyes are one option, not the fixed default expression.

Keep the mouth simple and expressive. Do not make the ω-shaped cat mouth the default; prefer a short curve, a small opening, or a structure that suits the creature.

[Colour, shading and volume]

Use a few organized colours and paint in large colour areas. Do not fix the palette to pastel or low saturation; use base colours that suit the character.

Each part centres on its base colour, with at most one broad, weak step of shading. Do not render with fine shading, many intermediate colours or smooth gradients.

Show volume with the curvature of the silhouette, overlap, perspective, foreshortening and colour areas wrapping round. Avoid strong lighting effects, excessive gloss and a 3D-render look.

[Boundary lines and hand-drawn feel]

Show forms mainly by the difference between neighbouring colour areas. For needed boundary and inner lines, use a colour of the same hue slightly darker than the fill of that part.

Where a part is close in colour to the background or a neighbour and its form gets lost, add a thin boundary line along the inside of its outline. Choose a colour in harmony with the fill: light grey for white, a slightly darker blue for pale blue, and so on.

Readable boundaries matter more than leaving out lines. But do not surround the whole body with a uniform thick black line, and do not add an outer rim or glow.

Very faint unevenness inside colour areas is allowed. Edges may keep subtle wobble, grain or chips. At normal viewing size, the flat colour areas and clear forms must read first.

Avoid strong grain, visible brush strokes, impasto, dirt, and drawn fur texture.

[Hair, clothes and ornaments]

Gather hair and fur into a few large smooth masses; do not add small strands or loose hairs.

For clothes, prioritize the silhouette, collar, sleeves, hem and main colours. Replace patterns and decorations with a few large shapes.

Leave out fine lace, embroidery, seams, metal fittings and complex patterns. Redesign clothes to fit the small body proportions.

[Pose and movement]

Even with a small body, give it a clear, expansive gesture.

Use the tilt of head, chest and pelvis, opposite slopes of shoulders and hips, twisting of the body, and the difference between the supporting and free leg. Make the centre of gravity and movement readable from the silhouette.

Do not cramp the spread of the arms, the reach of the hands or the step of the legs. Keep enough space between torso and arms and between the legs.

Exaggerate movement with angle, placement, overlap and foreshortening. Do not lengthen limbs or torso or complicate the joint outlines to show it.

[Priority when rules conflict]

1. A large head and a small body, 2.5 heads tall or less.
2. Not cross-eyed when looking ahead; no highlights in the eyes.
3. Simplicity of a few smooth shapes and flat colour areas.
4. Readable boundaries where needed.
5. Original design with a convincing structure, volume, an expansive pose.
6. A very subtle hand-drawn feel.
7. Details of clothes and ornaments.

When a detail conflicts with a higher rule, leave the detail out.

[Converting from a picture]

From the input picture, read the representative colours, hairstyle silhouette, main features of the clothes, held items, expression, face direction, gaze direction and the movement of the pose.

Keep the features needed to recognize the character, and rebuild it in the common style's proportions, shapes and drawing method. Do not carry over the source picture's proportions, realistic bones, small bumps or amount of detail.

Unless told otherwise, reinterpret a person as an animal or creature. Do not just add ears; design head, muzzle, hands and feet as one consistent creature. An existing animal or creature keeps its species and its core features.

Redraw the eyes in the common style. Remove highlights, reflections and iris gradients even if the source has them. Not having highlights and not being cross-eyed wins over how the source draws its eyes.

Keep the source's centre of gravity, body tilt, twist, arm spread and leg step, and exaggerate the characteristic movement moderately. Do not shrink the pose when reducing the proportions.

Organize the body outline into a few smooth masses, leaving out small bulges, constrictions and steps on the face and joints.

Keep the source's pale base colours. Where a part blends into the background, add a thin boundary line slightly darker than its inside colour.

When the picture to convert and a style reference picture are separate, keep them apart. Do not transfer the style reference's character-specific colours, hairstyle or ornaments.

[Extra conditions when drawing a character sheet or reference]

This picture is reference material for drawing the parts of a 2D animation. Readability and consistency come before presentation.

The background is a plain light single colour (a very pale blue-grey), with no shadows, floor, patterns, decorations or frames.

So that even a white character's forms read, draw a thin line at each part boundary in a colour of the same hue slightly darker than that part's fill. Do not surround the whole body with a thick black line.

In every figure on the same picture, keep the proportions, head size, colours, shapes and line widths the same, so it is obvious they are the same character.

For figures with a specified pose, the pose specification wins over [Pose and movement]. Do not add exaggeration, twisting or tilting that was not asked for.

In figures with the arms away from the body, leave a gap where the background shows between armpit and torso, so the shape of the torso under the arm is visible.

Never put highlights, white dots or reflections in the eyes. Not cross-eyed in front views.

Character notes, when given, have top priority. Do not remove or change features needed to recognize the character for the sake of the common style.
